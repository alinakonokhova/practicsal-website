What Is a Domain Name?

Learn how website addresses work, what domain extensions mean, and how to choose a name for your website.

Beginner friendly • About 8 minutes to read

In This Guide

What is a domain? Parts of a domain How domains work Domain extensions Registering a domain Choosing a name Domain vs. hosting Free domains Key takeaways

Every time you visit a website, you usually type an address into your browser. For example, you might enter google.com or wikipedia.org.

These addresses are called domain names. They make it easier for people to find websites without remembering complicated numerical addresses.

In this guide, we'll explain what domain names are, how they work, and what you should know before choosing one.

1. What Is a Domain Name?

A domain name is the human-readable address used to access a website on the internet.

For example:

https://example.com

Computers use numerical IP addresses to communicate across networks. Domain names provide an easier way for people to access websites.

Instead of remembering an IP address such as 192.0.2.1, visitors can type a name that is much easier to remember.

Think of it this way: A domain name is like the address of a house. It gives people a convenient way to find the place they want to visit.

2. What Are the Parts of a Domain Name?

Let's look at this example:

www Subdomain
example Domain name
.com Domain extension

A domain can contain several parts. Understanding them makes website addresses easier to recognize.

Domain name

The main name is the part you choose when registering a domain. In example.com, the name is example.

Domain extension

The extension is the ending of the domain, such as .com, .org or .net. It is also called a top-level domain (TLD).

Subdomain

A subdomain is an additional part placed before the main domain. For example, blog.example.com uses blog as a subdomain.

Website owners often use subdomains to organize different parts of a website, such as a blog, store or help center.

3. How Do Domain Names Work?

When you type a domain name into your browser, your computer needs to find the server that hosts the website.

This is where the Domain Name System (DNS) comes in. DNS is a system that helps translate domain names into information computers use to locate internet services, including IP addresses.

Here is a simplified example of the process:

01 Enter a Domain

You type example.com into your browser.

↓
02 DNS Lookup

Your device looks up the domain's DNS records.

↓
03 Find the Server

The browser gets the information needed to contact the website.

↓
04 Load the Website

The browser requests and displays the website.

DNS works behind the scenes. You usually do not need to think about it when visiting a website.

4. What Are Domain Extensions?

A domain extension is the ending of a domain name. Different extensions are available for different purposes.

Common domain extensions

  • .com — commonly used for commercial and general websites.
  • .org — often used by organizations and nonprofit groups.
  • .net — originally associated with network-related services, but now used more broadly.
  • .edu — used by eligible educational institutions under its registration rules.
  • .gov — used by eligible government organizations in the United States.

Country-code domain extensions

Some extensions represent specific countries or territories. These are called country-code top-level domains (ccTLDs).

Examples include:

  • .jo — Jordan
  • .uk — United Kingdom
  • .de — Germany
  • .fr — France

Some country-code domains have special registration requirements, so check the rules before choosing one.

Useful tip: A .com domain is a familiar choice for many general websites, but it is not automatically better than every other extension. Choose an extension that suits your website and audience.

5. How Do You Register a Domain Name?

To use your own domain, you normally register it through a company called a domain registrar.

A registrar is a company authorized to register domain names through the relevant domain registry or registration system.

Here is the usual process:

  1. Choose a name. Think of a short, memorable name that suits your website.
  2. Check availability. Search for the name through a domain registrar to see whether it can be registered.
  3. Choose an extension. Decide whether you want .com, .org, a country-code domain or another option.
  4. Complete registration. Follow the registrar's process and provide the required information.
  5. Renew your registration. Keep track of the expiration date and renew the domain when required.

Domain registration is generally for a limited period, often one year at a time. You do not normally buy a domain forever. You maintain the right to use it by renewing the registration.

Important: Check both the initial registration price and the renewal price. They can be different. Also check any extra fees and the registrar's renewal policies.

6. How Do You Choose a Good Domain Name?

Your domain name is an important part of your website's identity. A good name should be easy to remember and suitable for your project.

Keep it simple

Choose a name that is easy to spell, pronounce and remember. Very long or complicated names can be difficult for visitors to type.

Make it relevant

Your name should give visitors some idea of what your website is about, especially if you are creating a website for a business or a specific topic.

Avoid unnecessary characters

Hyphens and numbers are allowed in many domain names, but they can make a name harder to communicate. Use them only when they make sense.

Check for existing brands

Before registering a name, check whether it is already being used by another business or protected as a trademark. Registering an available domain does not automatically give you trademark rights.

Think about the future

Choose a name that can still work if your website grows or covers more topics later.

7. What Is the Difference Between a Domain and Hosting?

These two services work together, but they have different jobs.

📍

Domain

The address visitors use to find your website.

🏠

Hosting

The service that stores your website files.

For example, imagine that you register mywebsite.com and purchase a hosting plan from a hosting provider.

You then connect the domain to the hosting service by configuring the necessary DNS records or following the provider's setup instructions.

Once everything is configured correctly, visitors can use your domain to access the website stored on your hosting service.

If you want to understand hosting in more detail, read our guide: What Is Web Hosting?

8. Can You Get a Domain Name for Free?

Yes, but it is important to understand the difference between a free subdomain and a registered domain name.

Free subdomains

Some website platforms give users a free address under the platform's own domain.

For example, a website might use an address like:

mywebsite.platform.example

This can be useful for learning, testing and publishing a simple website without buying a domain.

Custom domain names

A custom domain is a name you register for your own use, such as mywebsite.com.

Most custom domains require payment, usually with a recurring registration or renewal fee. Some hosting or website plans include a domain for a limited period, but check the renewal terms.

Beginner tip: You can start with a free subdomain while learning. You do not need to purchase a custom domain before you understand how websites work.

9. Key Takeaways

Here are the most important points:

  • A domain name is the readable address used to access a website.
  • The DNS helps translate domain names into information used to locate internet services.
  • Domain extensions include .com, .org, .net and country-code extensions.
  • A domain registrar is a company that provides domain registration services.
  • Domain registrations need to be renewed to keep using the name.
  • A domain and hosting are different services that work together.

You now understand what a domain name is and how it helps people find websites. The next step is to learn how websites themselves work.
